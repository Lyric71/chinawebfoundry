# How to Hire a WordPress Agency in China

Picking a WordPress agency china partner is not like picking one anywhere
else. The build is the easy part. What actually breaks a foreign brand's
launch is everything downstream of the code: hosting, filings, and a
market that runs on Baidu instead of Google.

<!-- SECTION: Why a Generic Web Agency Isn't Enough for China -->
## Why a Generic Web Agency Isn't Enough for China

A WordPress developer in London or Chicago can build you a genuinely
beautiful site. Point a browser at it from Shanghai and thats often where
the trouble starts. Google Fonts wont load. Analytics calls time out. The
CDN you paid extra for gets throttled or blocked outright, because it sits
on the wrong side of the Great Firewall.

None of that shows up in a portfolio review. It shows up six weeks after
launch, when your China office cant reach the site and the agency that
built it has no idea why, they've simply never had a reason to find out.

Working inside mainland China means an ICP filing (备案) tied to the
domain and the hosting account, a server physically located in the
country, and a front end stripped of every dependency that calls a
blocked service. A generalist agency without hands on experience here
will burn your budget finding that out the hard way. A specialist
wordpress developer china already has the list memorised.

> Baidu holds roughly half of China's search market. Google sits under
> 3 percent. Source: Statcounter Global Stats, China, 2024.

## Must-Have Capabilities to Evaluate

Before you sign anything, check the agency against this list. Most shops
that claim "China experience" fail at least two of these.

| Capability | What to look for | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICP handling | In house filing, not a referral out to a third party | Filing sits on the critical path. Outsourcing it adds weeks |
| China CDN | Aliyun, Tencent Cloud, or Huawei Cloud, not a global CDN with a China node bolted on | Global CDNs still route around the Firewall, not through it |
| WeChat Pay (微信支付) and Alipay (支付宝) | Working payment integration they can demo, not just list on a slide | Chinese buyers rarely reach for a credit card at checkout |
| Bilingual support | A producer who reads and writes Chinese, on your call, not filtered through translation software | ICP paperwork, hosting panels and Baidu tools are Chinese only |
| Performance testing inside China | Load time measured from a mainland connection, not a VPN | A site that looks fast from your office can crawl from Shenzhen |

If an agency cant answer a row on that table with a specific example,
push for one. Vague answers here are the clearest signal you'll get
before you sign anything.

<!-- SECTION: Questions to Ask Before Hiring -->
## Questions to Ask Before Hiring

Past the checklist, a short conversation usually reveals whether an
agency has shipped a site inside the mainland or is guessing.

Ask them directly:

- Where is the hosting, and can I see an ICP filing on a live client
  site.
- What happens to Google Fonts, Google Maps and YouTube embeds during
  the build.
- Which Chinese payment gateways have you actually integrated, and for
  what kind of store.
- Can I talk to a current client hosted in mainland China, not Hong Kong
  or Singapore.
- Whats the plan if a plugin we depend on breaks behind the Firewall.

Red flags worth walking away from: offshore only hosting presented as
"good enough," no mainland testing before launch, and vague or evasive
answers on ICP. Nine times out of ten thats because the agency has never
actually filed one. An agency that hedges on hosting location is an
agency that hasnt done this before, no matter what the pitch deck says.

## Pricing and Engagement Models

Two models dominate. Project based pricing covers a defined build,
migration or redesign, with a fixed scope and a launch date. Retainer
pricing covers the ongoing work: content updates, Baidu SEO, plugin
patching, and monitoring once the site is live.

China specific work adds cost the West doesnt have, and its worth
budgeting for up front rather than discovering it mid project.

> ICP filing (备案) for an informational site typically takes 2 to 4
> weeks. A commercial ICP licence (经营许可证), required for transactional
> sites, runs 2 to 3 months. Source: ChinaWebFoundry client filing data,
> 2026.

A new WordPress build tuned for China generally runs 8 to 12 weeks from
discovery to launch. Migrating an existing site onto China compliant
hosting runs 6 to 10 weeks, mostly because the ICP filing has to run in
parallel with the technical work rather than after it. Ask any agency
quoting a flat four week launch how they intend to clear ICP in that
window, most cant, and the ones that skip the question are usually the
ones whose clients end up on Hong Kong hosting without ever being told
it was a compromise.

Retainer scope should spell out response times for Firewall related
breakage specifically, not just generic support hours. A plugin that
worked fine yesterday can fail today because a dependency it calls got
blocked, with zero warning from WordPress itself.
