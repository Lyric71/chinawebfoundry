How long a China website takes

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

How long does it take to build a website in China? With a mainland company
already registered, we plan fourteen weeks from kickoff to launch. Three
clocks run on every project: the entity, the ICP filing and the build. The
filing can't start until the entity exists, and the site can't open until
the filing clears, so those two run one after the other. The build runs
alongside the filing. Start the filing in week one and it finishes inside
the build. Start it when the design is approved and its three to six weeks
land on the end of the plan.

| Clock                 | What we plan                        | Overlaps with          | Can't start until                         |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Mainland entity       | Set by your parent company's papers | Scoping                | Apostilled documents from the parent      |
| ICP filing (ICP备案)   | 3 to 6 weeks                        | The build              | The entity, a mainland server, the domain |
| Build                 | 6 to 12 weeks                       | The filing             | A signed brief                            |
| Deploy and testing    | 2 weeks                             | Baidu account set-up   | The filing number and a finished build    |
| Baidu (百度) indexing  | 2 to 4 weeks after launch           | Public security filing | A live, filed site                        |

Every source below was checked on 17 October 2026.

<!-- SECTION: The three clocks -->

## The three clocks, and which ones overlap

Most project plans from outside China show one line: design, build, launch.
A mainland site has three, and the order between them sets the date.

The entity comes first because the filing is made in its name. The filing
comes second because the server can't serve the site until it clears. The
build can run beside both, on a staging copy away from the mainland server.

So the build is rarely the long pole. On a project with an entity, a filing
started in week one finishes while the developers are still working. On a
project without one, the entity and the filing stack end to end, and
together they usually outlast the build.

<!-- SECTION: The entity -->

## The entity clock: nothing files without a mainland company

The filing has to be made by a company registered in the mainland or by a
mainland resident. A foreign parent can't file for its own site from abroad.

> Alibaba Cloud international accounts do not support ICP filing. The filing
> entity must be an enterprise registered in the Chinese mainland or a
> Chinese mainland resident.
> Source: Alibaba Cloud (阿里云) help centre, ICP filing for enterprises
> outside the Chinese mainland, last updated 20 August 2026.
> https://help.aliyun.com/en/icp-filing/basic-icp-service/product-overview/icp-filing-application-for-enterprises-outside-the-chinese-mainland

The registration itself is short once the file is complete.

> A registration authority registers a complete application on the spot, or
> within 3 working days when it can't, with a further 3 working days for
> complex cases.
> Source: State Council Order 746, Regulations on the Registration and
> Administration of Market Entities (市场主体登记管理条例), article 19, 27 July
> 2021, in force 1 March 2022.
> https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/sczr1/20230508/3d4fe69e2cc04091a516be3483ef6357.html

The weeks go into getting the file complete. The parent company's documents
have to be certified at home first. Since China joined the Apostille
Convention, that means one stamp in the issuing country, with no trip
through a Chinese consulate.

> From 7 November 2023, public documents from other contracting states need
> only that state's apostille to be used in mainland China, with no consular
> legalisation.
> Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of China (外交部), announcement of 23
> October 2023.
> https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbxw_new/202310/t20231023_11165858.shtml

We don't put a number on this clock, because your lawyers and your home
registry set it. One detail matters for the website. Tencent Cloud (腾讯云)
requires a company site's content to fall within the business scope on its
licence, so write the scope with the site in mind.

## The ICP filing: three to six weeks, and what stalls it

The filing runs in two stages: the hosting provider's check, then the
provincial Communications Administration (省级通信管理局).

> Alibaba Cloud's initial review takes 1 to 2 working days. The provincial
> review generally takes 1 to 20 working days. The public security filing
> (公安备案) must be completed within 30 days of the site opening.
> Source: Alibaba Cloud (阿里云) help centre, ICP filing process overview,
> last updated 26 August 2026.
> https://help.aliyun.com/zh/icp-filing/basic-icp-service/user-guide/icp-filing-application-overview

> The provincial Communications Administration files a complete application
> and issues its number within 20 working days.
> Source: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工信部),
> Measures for the Filing of Non-commercial Internet Information Services
> (非经营性互联网信息服务备案管理办法), article 12, as amended 18 January 2024.
> https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html

On paper that's four weeks at most. We plan three to six, because the
paperwork before submission takes time and applications come back. The
things that send one back are mostly small.

| What stalls it                         | The rule                                                       | Source, date                     |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Server bought late                     | A mainland server on a subscription of 3 months or longer      | Alibaba Cloud, September 2026    |
| Domain at a registrar abroad           | Domains on alibabacloud.com move to a mainland registrar first | Alibaba Cloud, September 2026    |
| Domain holder doesn't match the entity | A corrected record takes 2 to 3 working days to reach MIIT     | Alibaba Cloud, September 2026    |
| Domain close to expiry                 | More than 45 days left before it expires                       | Alibaba Cloud, September 2026    |
| MIIT's text message unanswered         | Confirmed within 24 hours, or the filing starts again          | Alibaba Cloud, September 2025    |
| Regulated industry                     | Pre-approval from the sector regulator, medical devices included | Alibaba Cloud, August 2026     |
| Site live during review                | Closed or unreachable by its domain or IP                      | Tencent Cloud, August 2026       |

The last row catches teams who want a soft launch.

> During a new filing, the site cannot be accessed. It must be closed or
> unreachable by its public domain or IP, or it may fail the regulator's
> check and be rejected.
> Source: Tencent Cloud (腾讯云) documentation, ICP filing, last updated 21
> August 2026.
> https://cloud.tencent.com/document/product/243/19650

A site that charges users for information or online services needs the ICP
licence (ICP许可证) as well, and that clock is longer.

> The authority completes its review within 60 days of accepting the
> application.
> Source: Shanghai Communications Administration (上海市通信管理局),
> value-added telecoms service guide, 1 June 2015.
> https://shca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/dxsc/blcx/art/2020/art_7426922df3754a189aaf4278ff0c7b1d.html

We plan twelve to eighteen weeks for the licence. That figure is ours; the
regulator publishes only the 60 days, counted from acceptance.

<!-- SECTION: The build -->

## The build: six to twelve weeks, alongside the filing

We plan six weeks for a focused brand site and twelve for a larger one with
integrations and a full Chinese content set. None of it needs the filing
number. Design, development and Chinese copy all happen on a staging copy
off the mainland server.

What the build does need is a dependency plan in week one. Every call to a
host the Great Firewall blocks or slows has to come out, and that list
shapes the theme, the forms and the analytics. Our guide to moving
WordPress into China walks through that audit for an existing site.

The Chinese copy is the line that slips. Written properly, by a Chinese
writer, it takes longer than a translation, and it can't start until the
source copy is approved.

## Baidu indexing: two to four weeks after launch

Baidu (百度) can't index a site that isn't open, so its clock starts at
launch. We plan two to four weeks before the first pages show up. Baidu
publishes no timetable of its own, and its tools promise nothing on
indexing.

> Baidu took its fast inclusion (快速收录) tool offline on 26 April 2024 and
> replaced it with fast crawl (快速抓取), recalling the old quotas.
> Source: Baidu Search Resource Platform (百度搜索资源平台), notice of 24
> April 2024.
> https://ziyuan.baidu.com/wiki/3546

> Fast crawl is granted automatically to the platform's VIP partners. Other
> sites can earn it through platform activities. Submitted pages are crawled
> first, with no guarantee they are indexed.
> Source: Baidu Search Resource Platform, fast crawl guide, 19 April 2024.
> https://ziyuan.baidu.com/college/articleinfo?id=3549

A new site isn't a VIP partner, so it submits through the standard tools.
Real-name verify the Baidu account before launch, too.

> Baidu shut sitemap submission for sites in accounts without real-name
> verification, and for low-quality sites, and cut daily API push quotas.
> Source: Baidu Search Resource Platform, notice of 22 September 2023.
> https://ziyuan.baidu.com/wiki/3531

## How long it takes to build a website in China, week by week

This is the table the Shanghai team plans from, for a new site with the
entity already registered.

| Scoping and dependency plan                   | 1 to 2   | China account and server | A signed brief                              |

| ICP filing, submitted and reviewed            | 2 to 7   | Design, build, content   | The server, the domain in the entity's name |
| Design and build on staging                   | 2 to 12  | The filing               | The dependency plan                         |
| Chinese content                               | 3 to 12  | The build                | Approved source copy                        |

| Testing from mainland networks                | 13 to 14 | Baidu account set-up     | The deployed site                           |

Without an entity, add the entity clock in front of week 1. Scoping and

wait for the business licence.

## What shortens the timeline, and what doesn't

Four things save weeks. File in week one, against a server bought the same
week. Put the domain in the entity's name at a mainland registrar before
anything else, because the record takes days to sync. Choose the site's
Chinese name early and check it against the provider's rules. And keep the
person named on the filing reachable by phone for the whole review.

Some things don't move. The provincial review runs on the regulator's
clock. The site stays dark during a first filing, so there's no soft launch
on the mainland server and no coming-soon page on the domain. And Baidu's
fast lane went to VIP partners in 2024.

A smaller site does go faster. Our web agency in China page gives the eight
to twelve weeks we plan for a focused brand site with the entity in place.

weeks and budget lines before you sign.

## Frequently asked

**How long does it take to build a website in China?**

With a mainland entity in place, we plan fourteen weeks from kickoff to
launch, and eight to twelve for a focused brand site. The build takes six to
twelve weeks and the ICP filing three to six, running at the same time.
Baidu then takes two to four weeks to index the first pages, on our
planning.

**How long does ICP filing take?**

Alibaba Cloud publishes 1 to 2 working days for its own check and generally 1
to 20 working days for the provincial review. We plan three to six weeks,
because documents take time and applications come back. A site that charges
users also needs the ICP licence, and we plan twelve to eighteen weeks for it.

**Can we build the site while the filing is reviewed?**

Yes, and you should. Design, development and Chinese copy all happen on a
staging copy away from the mainland server. What has to wait is the deploy:
the domain stays dark until the filing number is issued, then the site goes
onto the mainland server with the number in its footer.

**Can we launch outside China while the filing runs?**

Not on the domain you're filing. Tencent Cloud's rule is that the site must
be closed or unreachable by its domain during a first filing. Your global
site on its own domain can stay up, and the China domain opens when the
number is issued.
